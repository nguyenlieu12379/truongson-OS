## The business, in one line
A Venezuelan pasapalos counter at 7868 NW 52nd St, Doral FL. Tequeños, empanadas, pastelitos, papitas and mandocas by the piece, party boxes of 50 and 100, frozen pieces wholesale. Orders on WhatsApp. 45.6K Instagram followers, 4.3 on Google from 260 reviews. No website.

## The eight topics
1. **Vibe** (authored, from their IG): golden fried food in white paper trays and open pizza boxes, a red sign, a busy counter. Warm, fast, generous. References: a Miami ventanita at 7 AM, the inside of a pastelería display case.
2. **Journey** (authored): the three pieces on their paper tray > the same pieces named and priced > the counter itself, shelf by shelf, with prices > party boxes and wholesale > why people come back > the storefront and how to order.
3. **Energy**: calm open, rising through the counter (the peak), steady through boxes, quiet close that holds.
4. **Feeling, stage by stage**: hungry > oriented ("I know what these are and what they cost") > there (standing at their glass case) > planning (a box for Saturday) > reassured (people love it) > ready (open now, here's the number).
5. **The one thing no other site does** (signature): their own shelf tags in their own counter footage turn into the menu. As you scroll the camera pans along the real display case, and a page label slides out of each printed tag (PAPITAS, EMPANADAS PAPA Y QUESO, PASTEL PAPA Y QUESO, MANDOCAS) with the price.
6. **Range**: serious counter-shop confidence. Not premium-minimal, not playful. Strong grotesque type, real photos large.
7. **World**: one unbroken fixed stage, zero cuts. Scroll only drives motion.
8. **Assets**: their Instagram reels and posts (frames at 1080x1920). No generated images.

## Grammar: Counter catalog (new)
A gallery/catalog grammar on one fixed stage. Every object gets the same label schema (name, what it is, price), and the labels belong to the real objects: pointed at the pieces in the hero, pinned to the printed shelf tags at the counter. Nav is an index of the objects. Bans: a single hero claim over footage, persuasion inside labels, novelty mechanics, cards floating on empty space, sections that scroll up.

Why not the others: filmic one-shot (taken three times, and the brief is a menu, not an argument); chaptered editorial (Vivace); continuous world with a z-camera (Delícias); stage choreography with a dealt hand (Pakachi); orbit (Sweets); clock (El Budare); live surface (not software); split stage (no two sides); cutlist (a counter is not an energy brand); typographic poster (the food is the asset).

## How this build avoids every FAILURES.md pattern
- **Picture + letter on a side for the whole page**: compositions change scene to scene (tray right, pieces centre with labels around, tall case frame centre with labels both sides, wide boxes, close-up, storefront + card).
- **Washed beige/grey, blurred photo fill**: neutral off-white ground, no blurred backgrounds; colour comes from the photos, their red in type, gold CTA.
- **Layers that don't relate**: every overlay points at something real (leader lines to pieces, labels anchored to tags).
- **Same small-label + big-sentence card every beat**: label schema only at scenes 2-3; other scenes use running copy or a price table.
- **Playful fonts and colour**: Instrument Sans (a confident grotesque) + Inter; no script, no cartoon face. Their sign's puffy letters are shown only as their photo.
- **Toggle stranded mid-bar**: toggle last in the bar, CTA right beside it.
- **Empty of value**: every scene answers something: what, how much, where, when, how to order, why.
- **Cutout never whole**: the three pieces are cut from a 1080x1920 frame where nothing is in front of them and nothing touches the frame edge; checked alone on white and black. The tray under them is a rebuilt clean plate.
- **Novelty mechanic as centrepiece**: the peak is their own counter footage, not a gadget.
- **Giant wordmark + cutout hero**: not used.
- **Blank space holding small UI pieces**: no chips or tickets; labels live next to the food they name.
- **Uncheckable names, invented times**: names come from their shelf tags and price graphics.
