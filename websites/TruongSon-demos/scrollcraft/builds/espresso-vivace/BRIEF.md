# BRIEF: Espresso Vivace (concept demo)

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.** Son said: "just pick the simplest one, find inputs online and build" (2026-11-20). No interview with the business. Every decision below is ours; facts are sourced, assumptions are labeled.

## The business (evidence)

- Espresso Vivace, Seattle. Coffee bar and roaster. Founded 1988 by David C. Schomer (former Boeing metrologist, USAF electronics tech) and Geneva Sullivan (DEC mainframe technician). Source: Wikipedia (Espresso Vivace; David Schomer).
- First incarnation: a coffee cart at 5th and Union. Then a covered stand near Broadway Market, then a roastery cafe on South Broadway, moved five blocks north for the light rail station. Source: Wikipedia.
- Schomer is credited with developing and popularizing latte art in the US. Fits grinders and espresso machines with PID controllers for constant water temperature. Published *Espresso Coffee: Professional Techniques* (1996), described as "the industry bible". Trained hundreds of baristas. Source: Wikipedia.
- Seattle Times quote: "Schomer is as influential in the gourmet coffee world as Starbucks CEO Howard Schultz is in the mainstream coffee industry." Source: Wikipedia citing Seattle Times.
- Schomer credits survival to "absolute fidelity" to "making a better cup". Source: Wikipedia.
- Broadway sidewalk bar closed May 2023 after 31 years. Open: Vivace at Brix, 532 Broadway E; Alley 24, 227 Yale Ave N. Source: closure notice photo (Commons), Wikipedia, web listings.
- Current site espressovivace.com: homepage is a looping espresso video with nav only. No headline, no story, no addresses or hours on the first screen. "visit" and "learn" render the shop. This is the pitch.

**Not verified (kept off the page):** hours, menu, prices, whether Schomer is still behind the bar daily.

## The eight topics (authored)

1. **Vibe:** exacting, warm, a little nerdy. References: a well-used lab notebook; a Criterion Collection booklet; the poster wall inside Vivace.
2. **Journey:** who they are, where it started, the obsession, the pour, the room, the book, where to go.
3. **Energy:** calm and literate for most of it. One intense held moment (the pour). Calm, useful ending.
4. **Feeling, stage by stage:** see the feeling curve. **The one moment:** you pour the rosetta yourself.
5. **What no other site does:** you draw a latte-art rosetta with your scroll wheel, then it wipes to a real Vivace latte.
6. **Distance from premium-minimal:** editorial. Paper grounds, folios, captions, real photos in columns. One loud ground (the yellow of their poster wall).
7. **One world or distinct scenes:** distinct chapters. Hard cuts between grounds.
8. **Assets:** 12 free-licensed photos from Wikimedia Commons (CC BY / CC BY-SA, credited on the page). No generated imagery. No spend.

## Grammar

**Chaptered editorial.** The story is substance (an engineer, a method, a book), so the visitor should feel they read something. Why the others lost: filmic one-shot needs footage we do not have; live surface is for software; continuous world needs a flight; typographic poster wastes real photos; gallery fits a product range, not a story; split stage has no two sides; rhythmic cutlist is the wrong energy for a 38-year-old espresso bar.

## Signature move

**The scroll-poured rosetta.** A pinned top-down cup. Scrolling pours a milk stream, builds the rosetta leaf by leaf from the bottom, then draws the pull-through line. The next chapter wipes the drawing into a real photo of a Vivace latte. Coded in the page (`site.js`), engine untouched.

## Tell-someone sentence

It's the site where you pour a latte-art fern with your scroll wheel, and then it turns into a real one from the cafe.

## The peak

"I scrolled and poured a rosetta, and then it became a photo of their actual latte." Lives in act 4. Gets the largest span (3.0vh), the only pin, and the silence of act 3 in front of it.

## Authored silence

Act 3 (intertitle) is deliberately near-empty: a dark ground with "III" and "The pour." Not dead scroll.
